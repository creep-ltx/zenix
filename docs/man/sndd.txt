SNDD(8)				Zenix manual

NAME
    sndd - the sound server

SYNOPSIS
    /sbin/sndd   (not a command: started by the first call to port
                 "audio", from /etc/svctab)

DESCRIPTION
    Owns Paula's four sound channels and plays what programs send it
    on port "audio" (play(1), modplay(1), and any program with the
    client library user/sndlib.c). It holds the kernel's claim of
    Paula: while it runs, the terminal bell is silent, and when it
    ends the kernel stops the channels before its memory is freed.
    Two kinds of stream:
      sound bytes   8-bit, mono or stereo, on a pair of channels (0
                    left and 1 right, or 3 and 2): two at once
      frames        a module's ticks on all four channels, from a bank
                    of samples in chip RAM: one at a time
    The channels are given to the first program that asks; another
    gets "in use" until they are free. Sound waits in chip RAM in two
    halves (60 ms each by default); sndd fills one while Paula plays
    the other, woken by the system's 100 Hz tick at times worked out
    from the E-clock, with no interrupts. A program that ends, or is
    stopped with Ctrl-C, has its sound stopped at once.
    sndd ends by itself after 30 s with nothing to play (the bell
    sounds again), and before ZINE takes the machine; the next call
    starts it again. Its priority is nice -10 (/etc/svctab).

FILES
    /etc/svctab   its line: audio -10 /sbin/sndd

NOTES
    With full memory protection (DESIGN D60 stage 4) sndd holds no
    register page: it hands the kernel its writes to Paula in batches
    (SYS_drv DRV_AUDIO), and the kernel checks that every channel plays
    only sndd's own chip RAM. Without protection (no MMU: the stock
    A1200) it writes the registers itself, as before, with no system
    call a buffer. play -S then shows DMACONR's audio bits only.
    Not done: mixing several sounds on one pair, priorities between
    programs, the bell played through sndd, 14-bit sound. sndd does
    not run while the kernel is busy for another program (a floppy
    track read): a gap in the sound unless the halves are longer
    (play -b). "play -S" shows its state, with the times Paula did not
    take a channel's new sample place in time; the first such time is
    also said on sndd's output.
