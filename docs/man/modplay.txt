MODPLAY(1)			Zenix manual

NAME
    modplay - play a ProTracker module

SYNOPSIS
    modplay [-v VOL] [-l] [-q] FILE

DESCRIPTION
    Plays a 4-channel ProTracker or NoiseTracker module (the tags
    "M.K.", "M!K!", "4CHN", "FLT4", or the old 15-sample kind without
    a tag) on Paula's four channels, through the sound server sndd(8).
    modplay works out each tick's periods, volumes and note starts;
    the server applies them at their time. The tempo is ProTracker's:
    F with a value below 32 the speed (ticks a row), from 32 the BPM
    (a tick is 2.5 / BPM s, the CIA timing).
    A line shows the module's name; then a status line, updated in
    place, the row being heard: the position, pattern, row, speed,
    BPM and each channel's last note and sample.
    The song ends when its order list has been played, at a jump back
    to an earlier position, or at F00.

OPTIONS
    -v VOL    the volume, 0-64 (64), applied to every channel
    -l        loop the song (F00 still ends it)
    -q        no lines and no keys: the terminal is left alone

KEYS
    q, Esc, Ctrl-C   stop
    space            pause, and go on
    n, p             the next, the previous position

EFFECTS
    0 arpeggio, 1 and 2 portamento up and down (to 113 and 856), 3 tone
    portamento, 4 vibrato, 5 and 6 (3 and 4 with a volume slide), 7
    tremolo, 9 sample offset, A volume slide, B position jump, C set
    volume, D pattern break, F speed and tempo; E1 E2 fine portamento,
    E3 glissando, E4 E7 vibrato and tremolo waveforms, E5 finetune, E6
    pattern loop, E9 retrigger, EA EB fine volume slide, EC note cut,
    ED note delay, EE pattern delay.

EXIT STATUS
    0 played; 1 not a module it plays (also one whose samples are
    over 1 MB); 2 no sound (no server, the channels in use, not
    enough chip RAM).

NOTES
    Not done: E0 (the filter: the machine's), E8 and EF; 6- and
    8-channel modules and other trackers' formats. ProTracker's
    behaviour on broken modules is not copied: an arpeggio past the
    period table plays its last note, a sample offset past the end
    plays the loop (or silence). Each tick is applied within about
    5 ms of its time (the system's 100 Hz tick); at 125 BPM, the usual
    tempo, exactly on every second tick. Keys are felt within the
    server's queue, about a third of a second. The whole module is
    read into memory, its samples copied to chip RAM (at most 1 MB).
