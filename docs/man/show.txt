SHOW(1)				Zenix manual

NAME
    show - the picture viewer of Zenix Desktop

SYNOPSIS
    show [-v] [-s | -w] [-n | -f] PICTURE

DESCRIPTION
    Opens PICTURE on a screen of its own (as AmigaOS viewers do), or with -w
    in a window on Zenix Desktop. The picture is an IFF ILBM (the
    Amiga's own format: 1 to 8 planes, Extra Half-Brite, HAM6, HAM8,
    24 planes; lores, hires, super-hires, interlaced; compressed or not)
    or a GIF (its first image). The file view, files(1), starts it for a
    picture.

    The picture has the shape it has on the glass: a lores picture is
    twice as wide in the desktop's hires pixels, an interlaced one or a
    GIF (square pixels) half as tall on a 256-line screen. A picture
    bigger than the window is shrunk to fit it; z shows it at its real
    size, moved with the arrows.

    The window draws in the desktop's 16 colours (8 on OCS/ECS: the
    theme's). A picture with other colours is dithered into them with a
    fixed 4 x 4 pattern, as strong as the colour is far from the
    nearest one (so a picture made of the theme's own colours shows
    exactly). d switches to Floyd-Steinberg (the error spread to the
    pixels right and below: finer, and slower), then to the nearest
    colour for each pixel, then back.

    n and p go to the next and the previous picture in the folder, by
    name without case; files that are not pictures and hidden names are
    passed over. A damaged picture is shown as its name and the reason
    in the window. The title is the name, the size and the mode
    ("ham.iff 320x64 HAM6").

    By default (and with -s) the picture is on a screen of its own
    (DESIGN D55): its own mode (lores or hires, interlaced, HAM6, HAM8,
    Extra Half-Brite) and its own colours, HAM in full colour. On that
    screen s, q, Esc or a click go back to the window (q there again
    quits); n, Space and p the next and previous picture there; the arrows
    (or h j k l) move a bigger picture (lines; across in steps of 64
    pixels). Left Amiga+N shows the desktop meanwhile.

KEYS
    n, Space               the next picture
    p, Backspace           the previous picture
    gg  G                  the first, the last
    z                      shrunk to fit, or the real size
    d                      pattern, Floyd-Steinberg, nearest, again
    s                      the picture on a screen of its own, or back
    h j k l, arrows        move a picture bigger than the window; Left
                           and Right are the previous and next picture
                           when the picture's width fits the window
    ?, Help                the keys (any key goes back)
    q, Esc                 quit (also the close gadget)

    The keys are read from ~/.config/show/keys, written with the
    built-in ones when it is missing. Its format is files'(1) and
    Ahiru's: one key a line, KEYS ACTION; the actions are next, prev,
    first, last, zoom, dither, left, right, up, down, help, quit, screen
    and none (a key taken away). A line not understood is said in the title.

MOUSE
    A left click shows the next picture, Shift and a click the
    previous. The bar's menu Show has Next, Previous, Fit or real size,
    Dither, Own screen, Keys and Quit.

OPTIONS
    -n      the nearest colour from the start (no dithering)
    -f      Floyd-Steinberg from the start
    -s      the picture on a screen of its own from the start (the default)
    -w      the picture in a window on the desktop from the start
    -v      write what it shows to standard output: the picture, its
            size and mode, where the window is, the rendering's time
            (the tests read it)

EXIT STATUS
    0 when the last picture shown could be read, 1 when it could not or
    there is no desktop, 2 a wrong option.

FILES
    ~/.config/show/keys    the keys

NOTES
    Not shown: PNG, JPEG, IFF's PBM, ACBM and DEEP, animations (an
    animated GIF shows its first frame), colour cycling, transparency.
    A picture is at most 4096 x 4096 and 4 MB decoded, a file 8 MB.
    In a window the picture has the desktop's colours; its own palette
    and mode need its own screen (the default). Not on a screen of its own:
    24-bit pictures and super-hires (no such mode), more than 32 colours on
    OCS/ECS, a desktop on a graphics card; show then uses the window and
    the title says why ("(no screen of its own: ...)"). A file
    cut short shows its whole rows and "cut short" in the title.
