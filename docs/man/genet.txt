GENET(4)		Zenix manual

NAME
    genet - the Ethernet port of a Raspberry Pi CM4 (or Pi 4) on a
    PiStorm

SYNOPSIS
    /sbin/net/genet IFACE [cache=kernel|cache=user] [noselftest]
                              (started by netd(8), never by hand)

    In /etc/network/interfaces:
        auto eth0
        iface eth0 inet dhcp
            driver genet

DESCRIPTION
    The driver of the CM4's own Ethernet port (Broadcom GENET v5 with a
    BCM54213PE PHY, 10/100/1000 Mbit/s) when Zenix runs on a PiStorm
    under Emu68. netd starts it for a stanza whose driver is genet and
    restarts it when it ends. It takes the chip's address, the PHY's
    address and the MAC address from the hardware table the boot read
    from Emu68's device tree, claims the chip (no other driver may touch
    it), gives the kernel a program that stops the chip's DMA (the
    kernel runs it when the driver or netd ends), resets the chip, and
    starts the PHY's negotiation.

    The chip writes received frames straight into netd's buffers and
    reads the frames to send from them, by DMA, in memory that belongs
    to the claim. The Pi's cache does not see the chip's DMA, so the
    driver cleans and drops cache lines around every frame: with
    Emu68's cache range instructions from the driver itself when the
    Emu68 on the card has them and they work outside the kernel (a
    child process tries them first), else through the kernel. A
    self-test then sends four frames to itself inside the chip and
    checks them byte by byte; if they come back wrong it uses the
    kernel's way, and if they are wrong that way too it refuses (the
    DMA could not be trusted). The log says which way it took.

    Polled (no interrupts yet): it looks at the chip again at once
    while frames move, then after 10 ms, and every 20 ms when idle;
    the PHY every 100 ms; netd's doorbell wakes it when there is a
    frame to send. The link's speed and duplex are set as the PHY
    negotiated them; ifconfig shows the link.

OPTIONS
    Words after "driver genet" in the stanza:
    cache=kernel   always the kernel's cache calls (skips the test of
                   the range instructions)
    cache=user     the range instructions without trying them first
    noselftest     no loopback self-test (if it fails on a chip that
                   works anyway)

PROBE
    The word "probe" (netd gives it when its card probe starts the
    driver for a card no stanza names, ifconfig(8) CARDS FOUND BY
    THEMSELVES): nothing is said until the hardware is there, so a
    machine without it hears nothing of this driver.

DIAGNOSTICS
    In the kernel log (dmesg), as "genet: IFACE: ...": "no GENET in
    the hardware table" (not a Pi 4 or CM4 under Emu68: it ends, and
    netd gives it up after five tries); "GENET v5.N at ADDRESS"; "PHY
    ID at MDIO address N (BCM54213PE): negotiating"; "trying Emu68's
    cache range opcodes in user mode in a child" and its answer (a
    child killed with vector 11 there is expected when they need the
    kernel); "self-test: 4 frames through the loopback, checked byte by
    byte" or what went wrong; "cache calls: ..."; "link up: N Mbit/s,
    full duplex", "link down"; "the send DMA is stuck" or "the receive
    DMA's index is wrong", then "the chip reset and started again";
    "the doorbell closed (ifdown, or netd is gone): the chip stopped".
    Every wait on the chip has a time limit and says what it waited
    for.

NOTES
    Not yet tried on the real A1200 (the owner's checklist is
    docs/a1200-checklist-network.md): tested on the PC against a model
    of the chip and the PHY (tools/genet-test.sh) and, in FS-UAE, only
    that it refuses cleanly where there is no GENET. No interrupts, no
    checksum offload, no multicast, no promiscuous mode, no jumbo
    frames (at most 1,500 bytes of packet). Only GENET v5 (Pi 4, CM4,
    Pi 400); a PHY that is not a Broadcom BCM54xx is used with its
    clock delays as it has them.
