NE2000(4)		Zenix manual

NAME
    ne2000 - an NE2000 PC Card (such as the Netgear FA411) in the
    A600/A1200's PC Card slot

SYNOPSIS
    /sbin/net/ne2000 IFACE    (started by netd(8), never by hand)

    In /etc/network/interfaces:
        auto eth0
        iface eth0 inet dhcp
            driver ne2000

DESCRIPTION
    The driver of an Ethernet PC Card in the side slot of an A1200 (or
    an A600): the Netgear FA411 (10/100 Mbit/s, an ASIX AX88x90 chip)
    and other NE2000 cards (a DP8390 chip or a clone). netd starts it
    for a stanza whose driver is ne2000 and restarts it when it ends.

    It claims the slot (no other driver may touch it), resets the
    card, reads the card's own description (its CIS) and takes it only
    if it says it is a network card, configures it, finds the chip,
    reads the card's MAC address and, on an AX88x90, its PHY (the link
    and its speed, 10 or 100 Mbit/s, half or full duplex, looked at
    every second). It gives the kernel a program that stops the chip
    and switches the card off: the kernel runs it when the driver or
    netd ends.

    Frames are copied between the card's 16 KB of memory and netd's
    buffers through the card's data port, 16 bits at a time. It is
    polled (no interrupts): it looks at the card again at once while
    frames move, then after 10 ms, and every 20 ms when idle; netd's
    doorbell wakes it when there is a frame to send.

    Taking the card out: the driver sees it, says so, and the link is
    down; frames to send are dropped (TCP sends them again). Putting
    the same card back: once it has stayed in for half a second, the
    driver sets it up again and carries on. A different card: the
    driver ends, and netd starts it for the new one.

PROBE
    The word "probe" (netd gives it when its card probe starts the
    driver for a card no stanza names, ifconfig(8) CARDS FOUND BY
    THEMSELVES): nothing is said until the hardware is there, so a
    machine without it hears nothing of this driver.

DIAGNOSTICS
    In the kernel log (dmesg), as "ne2000: IFACE: ...":
    "no PC Card slot (this is not an A600 or A1200)";
    "memory lies in the PC Card slot's addresses (0x600000-0xa4ffff):
    a card cannot be used on this machine" (the kernel's line before
    it, "drv: memory at A-B ...", says where the memory is); "no card
    in the PC Card slot"; "card: "MAKER" "PRODUCT", manufacturer M card
    C, function F" (what the card says of itself); "not a network card
    (function F): left alone"; "AX88790 at port 300 (configuration N,
    registers at R)"; "link up, 100 Mbit/s full duplex"; "the card left
    the slot"; "the card is back"; "starting the chip again".

NOTES
    No interrupts, no multicast, no promiscuous mode. 8-bit NE1000
    cards are refused. Cards with their MAC address elsewhere than the
    chip's PROM (some old cards keep it in their attribute memory) are
    refused when the address read is not a usable one.

    A machine with memory between 0x600000 and 0x9FFFFF (8 MB of
    Zorro II or some accelerators' memory there) cannot use a PC Card:
    the card and the memory answer at the same addresses. The kernel
    then refuses the slot; the card must not be put in while such a
    machine runs.

    Some A1200s with an accelerator do not start with a card in the
    slot: put the card in after power-on.

    Not yet tried on a real card: FS-UAE emulates no PC Card network
    card, so the chip is tested on the PC against a model, and in
    FS-UAE only what the driver does with an empty slot, a PC Card IDE
    disk and memory in the slot's addresses (tools/eth-test.sh p i m).
    DESIGN D36 (docs/d36-pcmcia-ne2000.md) has the first steps on the
    A1200.
