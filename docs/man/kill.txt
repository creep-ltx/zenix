KILL(1)		Zenix manual

NAME
    kill - end, stop or continue a process

SYNOPSIS
    kill [-s NAME | -NAME] pid...

DESCRIPTION
    Ends each process. -STOP (or -TSTP) stops it and -CONT continues it, as
    Ctrl+Z and fg do; every other signal name or number ends it, because
    there are no signal handlers yet. kill -l lists the names. The shell
    also has a kill that understands %n job numbers.

    Who may: root any process; everyone else only processes whose real or
    effective user id is their own (or their own children), never the
    built-in servers (diskd, fsd, zfsd, boot). A refusal says "permission
    denied". The same rule decides who may stop and continue a process and
    which processes a shell may make the foreground of its terminal.

    A process in the middle of a message call ends when the call is done.
    A server killed while it serves a request ends between requests, so a
    change on the disk is never cut in half; a second kill, or one still
    waiting after 5 seconds, ends it at once (a hung server). A service
    (diskd, the root's and the mounts' filesystem servers, those of
    /etc/svctab) killed this way is started again by the next call to it
    (DESIGN D11): kill is how root restarts one. dmesg shows the end and
    the start.

EXIT STATUS
    0, 1 if a process does not exist or may not be signalled.

SEE ALSO
